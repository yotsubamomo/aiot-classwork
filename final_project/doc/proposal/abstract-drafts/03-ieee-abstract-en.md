**Title**—Moto Touring Copilot: A Verification-Driven Multi-Agent Planner With Personalized Quantile Pace Modeling for Heavy-Motorcycle Touring

**Abstract**—Road traffic crashes killed 2,858 people in Taiwan in 2025, and motorcyclists accounted for 60.9% of these deaths, while registered large heavy motorcycles reached 279,703 by the end of the year. Before a mountain ride, a touring rider must decide when to depart to return before dark, where to refuel, and which segments are exposed to afternoon convection or typhoon-related road closures. Existing navigation services provide population-level travel-time estimates, motorcycle route-planning applications emphasize winding-road selection, and large language models (LLMs) remain unreliable as stand-alone planners, with GPT-4-Turbo achieving a final pass rate of only 0.6% on the TravelPlanner benchmark. This paper proposes Moto Touring Copilot, a multi-agent planning system that couples a personalized pace model with deterministic verification. A rider's 279 historical GPX trips are divided into 0.5–2 km road segments characterized by curvature, digital-terrain-model grade, road class, time of day, weather, and cumulative distance. A LightGBM quantile regressor predicts the logarithm of segment moving time at the 50th and 90th percentiles, and a conformal correction calibrated on held-out trips yields trip-level P90 durations and per-point P50/P90 arrival times. Five agents, comprising an orchestrator, a pace agent, a weather agent, a road-and-supply agent, and a verifier, retrieve Central Weather Administration forecasts, Transport Data eXchange road events, and fuel-station data at the predicted arrival time of each segment. The verifier enforces rules on daylight, fuel range, heavy-rain warnings, road closures, and numeric traceability, triggers replanning upon violation, and selects the departure time with maximum slack. The evaluation plan comprises trip-grouped cross-validation and temporal hold-out against mean-speed, speed-limit, and Google Maps two-wheeler baselines using MAE, MAPE, and P90 coverage; an ablation study scored against an independently computed oracle; a backtest of risk rules on historical closures; and case studies. All performance statements are hypotheses to be tested, and no reduction in crash rates is claimed.

**Index Terms**—Multi-agent systems, large language models, trip planning, travel time estimation, quantile regression, conformal prediction, GPS trajectories, motorcycles, intelligent transportation systems.
